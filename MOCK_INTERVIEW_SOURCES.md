# Mastery Interview Academy — Sources, evidence levels and learning policy

**Course:** [Mock Interview Academy](mock-interviews.html). This independent educational resource is not affiliated with Jane Street, Citadel, Citadel Securities, Meta, Google, Amazon, Apple, Netflix or Hudson River Trading.

## Why evidence labels matter

Mastery exposes one of three evidence levels **on each question**:

1. **Official published example:** a question or sample interview that the company itself published. Jane Street's [2017 retired “Memo” SWE interview](https://blog.janestreet.com/what-a-jane-street-dev-interview-is-like/) is an authentic published but **retired** interview problem; [its published SWE mock](https://www.janestreet.com/mock-interview/) and [trading mock](https://www.janestreet.com/trading-interviews/) are examples of collaborative interviewing. Practising them does not imply that they are still in the active bank.
2. **Community company tag:** a publicly compiled company-associated LeetCode title. The [company-wise CSV repository](https://github.com/liquidslr/leetcode-company-wise-problems) lists Citadel and Meta tags (snapshot last updated around 2025); [another public historical list](https://github.com/Faizi-alpha/LeetCode-CompanyWise-Problems) provides Google-tag examples. These tags are **not verified by the employer** and are not reliable per-role occurrence probabilities. The canonical problem page is linked; don't memorize a guessed frequency metric.
3. **Original practice:** an independent Mastery exercise motivated by published employer skill requirements, **not** a historical employer question. For example, the Amazon system-design drill follows its [SDE prep guidance](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/interview-prep/software-development-topics) and [Citadel's campus interview guide](https://www.citadel.com/careers/career-perspectives/our-engineering-interview-process/) supports the practice format, not the exact exercise.

## Copyright and interpretation

Mastery **does not mirror LeetCode/editorial statements, exact private questions, hidden tests, paywalled interview material or employer question banks**. It provides short, independently authored practice briefs and solution sketches with attribution links. A publicly accessible GitHub file is not proof that another site's copyrighted statements are available for unrestricted reuse.

Official employers explain process and evaluation, not a promise of a particular question. Community tags can be stale, inaccurate, or reflect internships/senior roles rather than a specific graduate opening. The student should learn transferable patterns, state uncertainty, and use multiple independent test cases.

## Structured assessment

Each interview has a timer, open-ended explanation and code area, concept glossary, progressive follow-ups, original model reasoning and five manual rubric dimensions: contract/clarification, correctness, complexity/trade-offs, edge cases/testing, and communication/follow-ups. Scores **are self-assessments**, not AI-graded proofs, verified code execution or hiring probabilities. C++ and JavaScript executable labs remain linked to separately test submitted code. Import/export preserves the user's practice record locally; no account synchronization or voice interviewer is implied.

## Proposed competency completion standard

Completing all lectures cannot guarantee success at any company. A practical readiness check should include: repeated unfamiliar mock rounds, a compiled tested solution for every coding case, measured improvement in weakest rubric dimension, independently correct follow-up reasoning, probability problems worked without hints, and at least two system-design defenses reviewed by an external mentor. Use a mix of new questions and spaced retention rather than memorizing an employer-labelled list.
